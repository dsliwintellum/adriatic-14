# Content and product notes

## Language positioning

The app labels the local mode **Croatian** in Croatia and **Montenegrin** in Montenegro. Shared phrases are reused internally, while locally preferred vocabulary is taught and recognized separately.

Montenegro’s constitutional framework identifies Montenegrin as the official language, recognizes Serbian, Bosnian, Albanian, and Croatian in official use, and gives Latin and Cyrillic scripts equal standing. The app teaches Latin-script production because it is the most practical travel interface for this itinerary.

## Curriculum method

The course uses complete conversational chunks rather than case-declension tables. It emphasizes:

- extracting the critical time, price, direction, or condition from a reply;
- asking for repetition or slower speech;
- locally preferred vocabulary without treating understandable cross-border variants as failures;
- transaction completion rather than accent elimination.

## Review status

This is a functional product prototype. The curriculum was assembled from standard Croatian/Montenegrin usage and travel-language patterns, but it has **not** received formal native-speaker editorial certification. Native Croatian and Montenegrin review is required before public sale or a claim of authoritative language instruction.

## Illustrative travel information

Named places match the user’s itinerary, but any schedules, prices, road conditions, travel durations, and spoken directions inside scenarios are fictional teaching examples. The app must not be treated as a live ferry, border, weather, navigation, or safety source.
